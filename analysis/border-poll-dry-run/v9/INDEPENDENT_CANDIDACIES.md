# The strongest independent candidacies in NI, 1970–2024

Phase 58. Follows from phase 57, where the independent vote turned out to be the single
largest confound in measuring bloc change (corr −0.576 with apparent bloc movement across
DEAs, worth up to 19pp in a single DEA). If independents distort results that much, it is
worth knowing where they have actually been strong.

Coverage: 66 NI contests, 1970–2024 — Westminster, Assembly, local government, European,
the 1975 Convention, the 1996 Forum. 1,550 independent candidacies, 508 elected.

## Definition, and why it decides the answer

| tier | labels | n |
|---|---|---|
| **A** — labelled independent | `Independent`, `Independent Unionist`, `Independent Nationalist`, `Independent Other`, `Independent (Name)` | 1,505 |
| **B** — non-party banner | `Anti H-Block` (Sands, Carron 1981), `Unity` (Devlin, McManus 1970) | 45 |
| **C** — excluded despite the name | `Irish Independence Party`, `Ulster Independence Movement`, `Ulster's Independent Voice` | — |

Tier B stood under an agreed label rather than a party — no manifesto, no organisation, no
common whip — and they are the largest results in the series, so a pure label test would
bury the very cases the question is really about. Tier C are parties; "independence" in
the title is about the constitutional question, not the candidate's status.

## Comparing across voting systems

Westminster is FPTP in single-member seats, so a share means what it looks like. Assembly,
local and European contests are STV in multi-member areas where the arithmetic ceiling on
any one candidate is far lower — a 6-seat DEA has a quota of ~14.3%. **Raw shares are not
comparable across the two.** STV results below are ranked by **quotas** (share × (seats+1)),
where 1.00 is election on the first count.

## Tier A, European (NI-wide, 3 seats)

John Gilliland 2004, 36,270 votes, 6.6% (0.26 quotas) — the highest; Bernadette
Devlin-McAliskey 1979, 33,969, 5.9%. No independent has come close to a European seat.

## NI-wide, over time

| | peak | latest |
|---|---|---|
| Local | **10.12%** (1973) | 4.61% (2023) |
| Westminster tier A | 6.33% (2010) | 3.03% (2024) |
| Westminster tier A+B | **18.46%** (1970) | 3.03% (2024) |
| Assembly | 4.37% (1973) | 2.93% (2022) |

The independent vote peaked at the start of the period and fell as the party system
consolidated — local government from 10.1% in 1973 to 4.6% in 2023, Westminster from a
combined 18.5% in 1970 (mostly Unity) to 3.0% in 2024. The one clear reversal is
Westminster 2010, at 6.33%, which is almost entirely Hermon and Connor.

## Two source defects found

- **1996 Forum**: the file carries a synthetic NI-wide row for the regional top-up list
  alongside the 18 constituencies. Those are the same ballots counted twice, and including
  it doubles the contest's valid poll (1,504,936 against the true 752,468). Phase 58 drops
  NI-wide/synthetic rows wherever real areas exist beside them; the European contests are a
  genuine single NI-wide constituency and are preserved.
- **January 1986**: titled "1986 UK general election in Northern Ireland" with
  `isByElection: False`, but it is the 15 simultaneous by-elections forced by the unionist
  MPs' resignations over the Anglo-Irish Agreement. Annotated in the output rather than
  edited at source.

## Caveats

- First preferences only. Under STV an independent's transfer performance often matters
  more than their first-count share, and none of that is captured here.
- Party labels are the source's. Some candidates recorded as `Independent` had a party
  history (Millar, Fitt, Hermon, Easton) and some nominal party candidates were
  independents in practice; no attempt is made to second-guess the label beyond tiers B/C.
- Nothing here is published. All outputs are local.
