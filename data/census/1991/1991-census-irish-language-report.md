1 1991 CENSUS - Prograraine of Census Reports 
Preliminary 
1.1 This report was published in July 1991 and contained provisional figures of the population and private households for 
each of the 26 local government districts with a brief statistical commentary. 
Summary 
1.2 The Summary Report was published in October 1992 and contains information for Northern Ireland and for each of the 
26 local government districts. The subjects covered include age distribution, sex, marital status, birthplace, religion, households, 
tenure, amenities, occupation density, availability of cars, communal establishments, economically active population, 
employment status, long-term illness, term-time address of students and schoolchildren, and Irish language. 
Belfast Urban Area 
1.3 The Belfast Urban Area Report was published in December 1992 and contains information to ward level for the Belfast 
Urban Area as defined by the Department of the Environment for Northern Ireland Town and Country Planning Service. The 
subjects covered include age distribution, sex, marital status, birthplace, religion, households, tenure, amenities, occupation 
density, availability of cars, communal establishments, economically active population, employment status, long-term illness, 
term-time address of students and schoolchildren, and Irish language. 
Religion 
1.4 This report was published in February 1993 and contains information at Northern Ireland level and some to local 
government districts on the main religious denominations. The subjects covered include population by age, sex, marital status, 
occupation, industry, and other characteristics. 
Economic Activity 
1.5 This report was published in April 1993 and contains tables giving the figures for both the economically active and 
economically inactive population. The employed population is analysed by industry and occupation classifications and relates 
to age, marital status, employment status, socio-economic groupings, area of workplace, etc. Most tables contain information 
at Northern Ireland level but some give details for local government districts. 
Workplace and Transport to Work 
1.6 This report was published in June 1993 and contains details of the employed population in relation to area of 
workplace/residence. Workplace movement by socio-economic group, occupation, industry, and means of transport to work are 
detailed to local government district level. 
Housing and Household Composition 
1.7 This report was published in September 1993 and contains tables giving information on households, tenure, occupation 
density, car availability, age, sex, marital status, and relate household composition to social and other economic characteristics. 
Most tables contain information at Northern Ireland level but some give details for local government districts. 
Migration 
1.8 This report was published in October 1993 and contains tables giving information on the numbers and characteristics 
of the population who changed their usual address in the year before Census day. Migrant households are detailed by age, sex, 
marital status, etc. Some tables refer to Northern Ireland and others are detailed to local government districts. 

Education 
1.9 This report was published in November 1993 and contains tables giving information at Northern Ireland level by age, 
sex, religion, occupation, and industry by highest level of academic qualification. Also included are socio-economic groups by 
religion and economic activity by marital status related to highest level of academic qualification. 
Irish Language 
1.10 This report contains information on population aged 3 years and over, recorded as having a knowledge of Irish language. 
The tables covered include knowledge by age, sex, religion, economic position, and highest level of academic qualification to 
Northern Ireland level.

2 Explanatory Notes and Definitions of terms used in the report 
Introduction 
2.1 For the first time, the Northern Ireland Census contained a question on Irish language. It was worded similarly to the 
Gaelic question in the Census for Scotland. The question asked whether persons aged 3 or over (bom before 22 April 1988) 
could speak, read or write Irish or whether they did not know the language. The exact wording of the question is shown at 
number 14 on the Census H Form which is produced at the end of this report. 
2.2 Tables contain information on knowledge of Irish related to other characteristics such as age, sex, marital status, religion, 
academic qualifications, economic position and social class. 
Administrative Divisions 
2.3 The local government districts for which statistics are presented in this report are those in existence at the date of the 
Census. 
Population 
2.4 Census results are presented as cross tabulations, each counting a particular population, for example, people, households, 
families, or people in communal establishments. It is important to know exactly what is being counted, i.e. the population base. 
Information on whereabouts and usual address given for each person listed on the Census form can be used to derive the 
population base. 
2.5 The population present on Census night (present population) in an area is the count of all people recorded as 
spending Census night in the area regardless of whether or not this was where they usually lived; that is, all persons recorded 
on the Census H Forms for whom the whereabouts on Census night was given as the relevant household, plus all persons 
recorded as being in communal establishments. 
2.6 The usually resident population in an area is the count of those people recorded on the forms for the area as usually 
living 'at this address' whether or not they are present on Census night. 
2.7 Shipping: Apart from houseboats (enumerated as private households), vessels were treated in a similar way to communal 
establishments with a listing form and individual return form for each person on the ship. Persons on naval vessels in Northern 
Ireland waters or ports on Census night were included as present in the home base port of the ship. Generally persons on other 
vessels were counted as present in the area in which the form was collected. 
2.8 For the 1991 Census there were some minor changes made to the enumeration of shipping. Vessels on coastal trips, 
fishing voyages and voyages between Great Britain and Northern Ireland were not enumerated. However, foreign registered 
ships in Northern Ireland ports, excluded in 1981, were enumerated. 
Campers, persons sleeping rough, etc. 
2.9 The present population also includes persons not enumerated in private households or communal establishments, for 
example, persons sleeping rough and persons enumerated in caravans or other non-permanent structures which were not the 
usual residence. 
Population covered in this Report 
2.10 Figures contained in this report refer to the population aged 3 years and over who were usually resident in Northern 
Ireland. 
Irish Language 
2.11 The question on Irish language asked whether persons aged 3 years or over could either speak, read or write Irish. It 
also contained a category for those with no knowledge of Irish. 

Religion 
2.12 It was not obligatory to answer the question on religion. The question was worded as follows: 
Please,state the Religion, Religious Denomination or Body to which the person belongs. The term Trotestant' should not 
be used alone and the denomination should be given as precisely as possible. If none write NONE. 
2.13 The classification of religions includes a separate heading for each religion. Religions which differ only or mainly in 
denominational terminology are generally grouped together and the figures for the group as a whole are shown under the name 
by which the group is officially or commonly known. Thus the heading Presbyterian includes United Presbyterian, Trinitarian 
and Church of Scotland. Church of Ireland is regarded as embracing both Anglican and Episcopalian except for persons so 
described whose usual address was in England: in such cases the Church of England classification was applied. 
2.14 Also included was a heading for persons who stated they had no religion. 
Age 
2.15 Age is the completed years of age at Census date, as derived from the date of birth question. 
Usual Address 
2.16 In most cases thej answer to the usual address question is straightforward. However, students and schoolchildren away 
from home during term-time were to regard their home address as their usual address. Boarders were to decide whether they 
considered as their usual, address, the address of the relevant household. Household heads, living av^ay from home for part of 
the week, were to regard their home address as their usual address. 
2.17 Persons who failed to answer the relevant questions appear as part of the usually resident population in the area in which 
they were enumerated and are included as part of a usually resident private household. 
Economic Activity "^ 
2.18 The economic activity tables describe some of the economic characteristics of the population. The characteristics include 
both those obtained directly from census questions, for example, economic position, and occupation, and others derived from 
combining answers to census questions on occupation and industry, for example, employment status. Persons on a Governments 
employnaent or training scheme are regarded as economically active in employment. However, while information about the 
particular scheme was not collected in the Census, persons on these schemes are included as a separate group in tables which 
give details on economic position and occupation. 
2.19 Only specified groups were required to answer some of the economic activity questions and this is reflected in the 
population to which tables refer. Thus: 
(a) Tables relating to economic position include only persons aged 16 years and over. ......^ 
(b) Tables relating to occupation cover the economically active. 
(c) Tables relating to employment status cover persons in employment.

Economic Position 
2.20 The classification by economic position is as follows: 
Economically attive 
In employment rheans having a job during the week ended 20 April 1991. Temporary, part-time or casual employment 
- is included. Persons absent from their employment due to holiday, strikes, lock-outs, short-time working or temporary 
stoppages are regarded as in employment. Persons off work sick are regarded as in employment if their jobs are waiting 
for them on return. 
Out of employment means seeking work or prevented by temporary sickness from seeking work or waiting to take 
up a job already accepted. 

Economically inactive 
Looking after the home means having full-time responsibility for looking after the home. 
Long-term sick/disabled means being unable to work because of long-term sickness or disability. 
Retired means wholly retired from employment and no longer seeking paid work. 
Students are persons in full-time attendance at an educational establishment irrespective of whether or not they may 
have part-time or seasonal employment. 
Others are persons economically inactive and include persons in unpaid work in a family business including a shop 
or farm, or looking after the house or family, or in voluntary work, or never in employment and not seeking work, and 
those of independent means. 
Employment status 
2.21 The classification of employment status is as follows: 
Self-employed 
1. With employees. 
2. Without employees. 
Employees 
1. Managers 
(a) extra large establishments. 
(b) large establishments. 
(c) small establishments. 
2. Foremen and supervisors * 
(a) manual. 
(b) non-manual. 
3. Professional employees. 
4. Other employees. 
2.22 Information on occupation is used to distinguish the various types of employees and information on name and business 
of employer to distinguish the size of the establishment. Extra large establishments are those classified as having 500 or more 
employees. Large establishments are those having between 25 and 499 employees. Small establishments are those with fewer 
than 25 employees. 
Classification of Occupations 
2.23 The occupation of a person is most often determined by reference to that person's main job at the reference time, but 
for persons not currently employed it may be determined by reference to the most recent, or most recent main, job. Any given 
job is assumed to involve a set of typical work activities and to be an instance of a particular occupation. 
2.24 Occupation codes are allocated from the answers to question 17 on the Census form. In the tables, occupations are 
classified according to the Standard Occupational Classification (SOC) 1990 issued by the Office of Population Censuses and 
Surveys and published by HMSO under ISBN 0 11 691284 7 (Vol 1), ISBN 0 11 691285 5 (Vol 2) and ISBN 0 11 691338 
X (Vol 3). 
Note: SOC 90 contains new classifications which may differ from those contained in the Classification of Occupations 1980 
(CO 80) which applied to answers given to question 13 on the 1981 Northern Ireland Census form. 

Principles of the classification 
2.25 The object of the classification is to group together occupations (basically by reference to job titles) which are deemed 
to be similar, taking simultaneous account of 2 main criteria. These criteria are: 
i. the level of skill and/or experience and/or formal qualification which is required to carry out competently the work 
activities typically involved in the occupation; and 
ii. the nature of those work activities. 
2.26 The first criterion - skill level - is applied where possible by reference to the level of formal qualifications currently 
required in order for a person to be recognised as fully competent in the occupation concerned; but in other cases by reference 
to the duration of training and/or experience recognised in the field of employment concerned as being normally required to 
pursue the occupation competently. 
2.27 The second criterion - nature of work activities - may refer to any or all of a number of aspects of the work, including 
the materials handled, the type of tools and equipment used, and so on. However, it focuses specifically in all cases on work 
activities, rather than on other aspects of the job such as, for example, whether the worker has trainee or supervisory status, 
or what industrial function the job serves. These are ignored by the SOC, - so that, for example, a 'carpenter', an 'apprentice 
or trainee carpenter' and a ^foreman carpenter' are all assigned to exactly the same occupational group. 
Treatment of managerial occupations 
2.28 An area where j the nature of the activities, type of skill and status in employment may be difficult to separate 
conceptually is management. The SOC approach, is to treat jobs where the main activity is, 'management above the level of 
a first-line supervisor or foreman', as requiring a special class of skills and thus needing to be classified separately from the 
jobs of the workers managed. Thus in allocating jobs to occupational groups the SPC takes account, in appropriate and defined 
cases, of the designation 'manager' and also of indications that the person is the proprietor of a business. These are treated as 
indications that the job involves activities and skills typical of managers. v. 
2.29 On the same principle there are a few cases where allocation to an occupational group may be assisted by knowledge 
of whether or not the person has self-employed status. In these limited respects, information on status in employment is taken 
into account in the SOC. 
Treatment of labouring and similar unskilled occupations 
2130 Jobs having the title 'Labourer' are in general classified, along with other jobs requiring relatively little skill, 
qualification or experience, to Major Group 9 of the SOC. Because of the unspecific terms in which many such jobs are 
reported in practic^ it is necessary to invoke a quasi-industrial principle for classifying below major group level. This gives 
rise to minor group headings such as: 
'91 Other occupations in mining and manufacturing'. 
'92 Other occupations in construction', and so on. , 
2.31 The ultimate recourse is to Minor Grotip 99 and Unit Group 990 which is a catch-all for 'All other labourers and related 
workers'. The practical implications of this are tending to diminish as the number of generalised unskilled jobs in the economy 
continues to decline. 
Treatment of Armed Forces occupations 
2.32 Members of the Armed Forces may in certain cases have jobs which are unique to that sector of the economy. The SOC 
makes provision for military occupations specified in terms of ranks (e.g. 'Lieutenant Commander', 'Sergeant' etc.) in Minor 
Groups 15 (Commissioned Officers) and 60 (NCOs and other ranks). In other cases job titles, if given, may equate to those 
of civilian jobs (e.g. vehicle mechanic, radio operator etc.) though of course the persons concerned all hold military ranks also. 
Here the basic classificatory assumption is that both Armed Forces and civilian jobs will be coded to the same groups without 
any distinction being made between them. 

Treatment of agricultural, forestry and fishing occupations 
2.33 Occupations which are specifically agricultural, forestry and fishing, but not managerial, in nature are allocated to Minor 
Group 90 of SOC and its constituent Unit Groups. This group of occupations is sufficiently distinct from other occupations 
to require its own small set of minor groups and might therefore claim its own major group. However, one criterion for 
distinguishing major groups was that each should contain a substantial proportion of all occupations, and the proportion of the 
labour force currently engaged in agriculture, forestry and fishing is now so small that major group status was judged not to 
be appropriate. 
Social Class 
2.34 The groups in the classification of occupations are arranged into a small number of broad categories called Social 
Classes. Economically active persons are assigned to one of the following Social Classes by reference to their present or former 
occupation: 
I Professional etc., occupations. 
II Managerial and technical occupations. 
III Skilled occupations: 
(N) non-manual. (M) manual. 
IV Partly skilled occupations. 
V Unskilled occupations. 
2.35 The occupation groups included in each of these categories have been selected in such a way as to bring together, as 
far as possible, people with similar levels of occupational skill. In general, each occupation group is assigned as a whole to 
one or other Social Class and no account is taken of difference between intjividuals in the same occupation group e.g. 
differences of education or levels of remuneration. However for persons having the employment status of foreman or manager, 
the following rules apply: 
(a) each occupation is given a basic Social Class. 
(b) persons of foreman status whose basic Social Class is IV or V are allocated to Social Class III. 
(c) persons of manager status are allocated to Social Class II with certain exceptions. 
Academic Qualifications 
2.36 Academic qualifications have been classified into 7 levels as follows: 
1. Degree level or higher. 
2. BTEC (Higher), BEC (Higher), TEC (Higher), HNC, HND. 
3. GCE 'A' Level, Advanced Senior Certificate. 
4. BTEC (National), TEC (National), BEC (National), ONC, OND. 
5. GCSE, GCE 'O' Level (including CSE grade 1), Senior Certificate, BTEC (General), BEC (General). 
6. CSE (other than grade 1). 
7. No formal qualifications as above. 
Presentation Conventions 
2.37 A dash (-) indicates that the entry is zero. A blank indicates that a particular combination of line and column is 
impossible. In the presentation of rates, a rate shown as 0 indicates that the actual rate is less than one half of a unit but is not 
zero.
